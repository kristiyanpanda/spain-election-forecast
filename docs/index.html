Forecasts made 30 days before each election with only the polls available then. The model's 90 % vote intervals covered 40 % of outcomes, so the election-day projection adds an error of 12 % of each party's share, the smallest value that would have restored 90 % coverage; mean reversion toward the last result was tested (rejected). The regional-poll layer enters the backtest only for Catalonia in 2023 (CEO), the one case with regional data.

Method in one paragraph

Official results 1977-2023 come from the Ministerio del Interior; polls from the Wikipedia opinion-polling tables (CC BY-SA 4.0); CIS straight from cis.es: its official estimate as one series, and our re-estimate from its microdata (2023 recall, likely voters) as another, with series built from the same survey sharing its sample size. A pollster's releases with overlapping fieldwork (daily trackers, cumulative panels) count once. The aggregator is a state-space model on a weekly grid: each party's share follows a random walk on the logit scale, anchored on the July 2023 result, with hierarchical house effects per pollster and a noise term that combines sample size with a party-specific excess variance. Seats are simulated by proportional swing from each party's 2023 provincial share and calibrated provincial noise. Regional surveys (CEO in Catalonia, the Basque Sociómetro, CIS subsamples in Galicia) then pull the peripheral parties' regional shares toward them. The 29N ballots follow the lists proclaimed on 3 November: Frente Amplio here includes Compromís-Front Valencià and its joint lists in Canarias and Baleares; Podemos has no share in the ten provinces where it has no list (Catalonia, where its candidates run with ERC, the Valencian provinces, Canarias and Baleares); UPN runs with PP. D'Hondt is applied in the 52 districts (3 % threshold; Ceuta and Melilla by plurality). Sampling: NUTS, 4 chains x 1,000 draws, 0 divergences, R-hat 1.00.
